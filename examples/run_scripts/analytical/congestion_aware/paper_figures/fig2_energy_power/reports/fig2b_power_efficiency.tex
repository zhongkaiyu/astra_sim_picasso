Fig.~\ref{fig:power_efficiency} compares the power efficiency (Token/J) of our proposed architecture against Rubin, H100, and their TP2 variants across varying sequence lengths on Qwen3-235B and Llama4-Maverick.
Our architecture achieves 2.5--4.1$\times$ higher energy efficiency than Rubin~TP2 across all evaluated configurations.
At short context lengths (seq=4K), the advantage is most pronounced: our design reaches 305~Token/J on Qwen3-235B and 330~Token/J on Llama4, compared to 74 and 76~Token/J for Rubin~TP2 respectively, representing a 4.1--4.4$\times$ improvement.
As sequence length increases to 1M, the gap narrows to 2.5$\times$ due to the growing dominance of attention computation, which scales linearly with context and is bounded by per-NPU HBM bandwidth.
Compared to H100~TP2, our architecture delivers 6.5--8.0$\times$ higher Token/J, primarily because the aggregate HBM bandwidth of 16~NPUs (40~TB/s) is 11.9$\times$ that of a single H100 (3.35~TB/s), enabling proportionally faster completion of memory-bound decode operations.
Notably, H100~TP2 shows marginally \emph{lower} efficiency than H100~TP1 at short sequences, as the NVLink communication overhead is not fully amortized when compute time is small.
These results are consistent across both model architectures, confirming that the efficiency advantage is architecture-driven rather than model-specific.
